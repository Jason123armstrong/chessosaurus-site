Privacy policy

Chessosaurus is made for children. It asks for no personal information about you or your child, and nothing is sent to us unless a grown-up chooses to send it. Last updated 26 November 2026.

In short: no accounts, no email, no adverts, no analytics and no tracking. Your child’s players, progress and saved games stay on the device. Nothing is sent to us unless a grown-up chooses to send it: an optional error report, which a grown-up switches on, or a grown-up’s own email with a suggestion and the child’s game ratings. If two children play on two devices, only the chess moves (and the messages that keep the game in step) pass between those two devices, encrypted, over your home Wi-Fi. On Google Play the app is free to try for 3 days; after that a grown-up can unlock it once through Google Play, and the app asks Google Play whether it has been unlocked. On the Amazon Appstore it is bought once from Amazon before it is downloaded, and it contains no payment code (see “Buying Chessosaurus” below).

What stays on the device

The app never asks for a name: each player is simply their favourite dinosaur. Favourite dinosaurs, progress, stars, stickers, settings and saved games are kept only on the device. We never receive them. Progress and settings are not included in device backups, do not move to a new device, and are deleted with the app. Saved games are files in the device’s Documents folder (on Android 10 and later), where a grown-up can see them in the Files app; they stay there if the app is removed, until someone deletes them. On older Android versions they are kept inside the app and deleted with it.

What we collect

Nothing, unless a grown-up chooses to send it: an error report (see below) or an email with game ratings (see “Game ratings”). The app has:

  • no accounts or sign-in, and it never asks for a name, email address or phone number;
  • no adverts and no advertising identifiers;
  • no analytics, tracking or crash-reporting services from other companies;
  • no chat, and no way for children to send messages or share personal information;
  • no location access.

Game ratings

After finishing a level of a game, a child can say whether they liked it: thumbs up or down, “More like this!” and up to five stars (hearts for younger players). Ratings are kept on the device, for each dino (player) separately. They are never asked for and never rewarded, and a grown-up can clear them in the grown-ups area.

Ratings reach us only if a grown-up chooses to send them. In About, behind a grown-up check that can’t be switched off, “Send a suggestion” opens the grown-up’s own email app with a message they can read, change or delete before sending. The message holds only the app’s version, the store, the dinos’ names in the app and how each game was rated. It has no child’s name, no age and nothing about the device. Nothing is sent unless the grown-up presses Send in their email app.

If you send it, we also receive your email address. We use the message only to decide which games to improve or add, on the basis of your choice to send it, and we delete it after 6 months. You can ask us to delete it sooner at privacy@cumbernauldsoftwaresolutions.com.

Optional error reports

Error reports are off unless a grown-up switches them on, behind the grown-up check, and the internet setting is also on. When switched on, one report is sent the next time something goes wrong, and error reports then switch themselves off again.

A report containsA report never contains
The app's name, version and type; the Android version and device model; the language; which screen of the app was open; what went wrong inside the app (the type of error, a short message and the app's own code lines); how long the app had been open. Names, email addresses, phone numbers, contacts, location, photos, device identifiers or anything typed.

Reports are stored in London, in the United Kingdom, in Google Cloud Firestore, which we use only to receive them. Google stores them for us as our processor, on our instructions. They are deleted after 30 days. While the internet is off, a report waits on the device and is deleted after 7 days if it can't be sent. Switching error reports off deletes any report still waiting, and is as easy as switching them on: it is the same switch.

Like any message sent over the internet, a report arrives from the network address of the connection it was sent from. We don't add that address to the report and we don't keep it; Google, which runs the servers, handles connection information under its own policies.

Buying Chessosaurus

On Google Play, Chessosaurus is free to try for 3 days. The 3 days start the first time the app is opened. After that a grown-up can unlock it once through Google Play: one payment, no subscription, and nothing is ever charged automatically. Children never see a price or a buy button: the price is shown only behind a grown-up check that can’t be switched off.

Google Play takes the payment: we never see your card details or your Google account. The unlock belongs to the Google account used to buy it and covers every player on that device. On another device, install the app from Google Play using the account that bought it, then choose “Restore my purchase” if you need to.

Each time the app starts, and when a grown-up buys or chooses “Restore my purchase”, the app asks Google Play whether the unlock has been bought with the Google account on your device. This is the one thing that does not stay on your device, and Google Play does it even when the app’s own internet setting is off. Google Play’s payment code inside the app can also send Google technical details about the purchase and the device, such as its model, its country and the app’s version. Google handles that information under its own privacy policy.

On your device the app keeps: whether it is unlocked; when the 3 free days started and how much of them has passed (for this it also notes how long the device has been switched on, and Android’s own count of how many times it has been switched on, which is a plain counter and does not identify the device); what Google Play last answered; and, for a purchase Google has not yet been told arrived, a short one-way fingerprint of it, never the purchase’s own code. None of that is sent to us.

On the Amazon Appstore (Fire tablets), Chessosaurus is bought once from Amazon, before it is downloaded. There is no free trial and nothing to buy inside the app: the Amazon version contains no payment code at all. Amazon takes the payment under its own terms and privacy notice; we never see your card details or your Amazon account. The Amazon version never asks Amazon or Google Play about a purchase, so the paragraphs above about Google Play do not apply to it: everything you and your child do in it stays on the device, apart from the optional error report and email described in this policy.

Playing on two devices

Two nearby devices can be paired for a game. A grown-up scans a code shown on one device with the camera of the other. The camera only reads that code, on the device: no picture is taken, kept or sent. The pairing code itself is never sent. Once paired, only chess moves and the messages that keep the game in step are exchanged, encrypted, directly between the two devices over your own Wi-Fi network. Both devices need to be on the same Wi-Fi. Nothing goes through our servers or the internet. A grown-up can switch two-device play off.

Reading aloud

The app can read questions, lessons and names aloud. It uses the voices already on the device, working offline: nothing your child hears or taps is sent anywhere to make the speech. A grown-up can add a voice for another language from the device's own settings, behind the grown-up check.

What the internet is used for

  • By the app: optional error reports only, as described above. The app's internet setting starts off. (A ratings email is sent by the grown-up's own email app, not by Chessosaurus.)
  • By Google Play (Google Play version only): each time the app starts, and when a grown-up buys or chooses “Restore my purchase”, the app asks Google Play whether it has been unlocked. This does not use the app’s internet setting: Google Play does it either way. See “Buying Chessosaurus”.
  • By the device's browser: if a grown-up opens a web link, it opens outside the app.
  • By the device's own settings: if a grown-up adds a voice for reading aloud, the device downloads it once.
  • Not the internet: two-device play uses your own Wi-Fi network only.

Links and sharing

Web links (including the store, this website and this policy) and sharing always ask a grown-up first, even if other checks are skipped. If a grown-up opens a link, it opens outside the app, and that website's own privacy policy applies.

Children's privacy

Chessosaurus is designed for children, with the UK Age Appropriate Design Code (Children's Code), the UK GDPR, the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) Google Play's Families policy and the Amazon Appstore's policies for children's apps in mind. Because we collect no personal information from children, there is nothing for us to share, sell or use for advertising.

Your rights

You can ask us about anything in this policy, or ask us to delete an error report, by emailing privacy@cumbernauldsoftwaresolutions.com. Reports cannot be linked to a person, so please tell us roughly when it was sent and on which device model. You can also complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

This website

This website uses no cookies, no analytics and no tracking, and loads nothing from other websites. The share buttons and the “follow” links (Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, TikTok and X) are plain links: nothing from those sites loads unless you click one, and then that site's own privacy policy applies. It is hosted on GitHub Pages; GitHub may keep standard server logs, including visitors' network addresses, for security, under its own privacy statement.

Changes

If we change this policy, we will update this page and the date at the top. We will never start collecting personal information from children.
