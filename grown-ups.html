For grown-ups: privacy, safety and how Chessosaurus works

Everything a parent, carer or teacher needs to know about Chessosaurus, the chess app for ages 6 to 12. Thank you: keeping children safe is our top priority.

The grown-up check

Anything that changes an important setting, or could leave the app, asks for a grown-up first. Every check shows the same line, so children learn what it means:

“Thank you. Keeping children safe is our top priority.”

There are two kinds of check:

  • A short sum. Hold a button for a moment, then answer a sum.
  • Your own 6-digit PIN, if you set one in For grown-ups. It never leaves the device and there is no account behind it. Forgotten it? A grown-up can reset it with the device’s own screen lock, or with a sum and a 24-hour wait, and the app says when that has happened.

These always ask a grown-up, even if checks are skipped:

  • store, website and privacy links, and sharing the app or a game;
  • sending game ratings to us;
  • turning the internet on, or turning on error reports;
  • playing on two devices, and pairing them;
  • deleting a saved game, or removing a player;
  • opening a dino that has grown-up extras (below).

If you are happy for an older child to manage the everyday settings, you can skip the other checks, for every dino or for one dino at a time. The app lists what will still ask before you choose.

Grown-up extras for each dino

Extras such as skipping checks, links, the internet or error reports can be switched on for one dino only. A dino with extras switched on opens only after the grown-up check, so a younger child can’t pick it by mistake.

The For grown-ups settings

Tap ⚙ Settings on the home screen (or ⚙ above the board), then the For grown-ups tab. There you can:

  • see each child's progress;
  • choose whether the internet is used at all;
  • switch the optional error reports on for one report;
  • switch two-device play off or on;
  • set or change a grown-up PIN, and switch on extras for one dino;
  • delete saved games (players are kept).

The internet and error reports

Chessosaurus works fully without the internet, and the internet setting starts off. Nothing is sent to us unless a grown-up chooses to send it: an error report, or an email from your own email app with a suggestion and your child’s game ratings (see the privacy policy). Two other things can use a connection: two-device play sends chess moves between your own two devices over your home Wi-Fi, and, once Chessosaurus is on sale, Google’s own payment code talks to Google Play (the test version has none). See the privacy policy.

Error reports: one at a time

If something goes wrong in the app, an error report helps us fix it. Reports are off unless a grown-up switches them on, and then only one report is sent: the next time something goes wrong, one report goes and error reports switch themselves off again. The app tells you this when you switch them on.

A report holds the app's name and version, the Android version, the device model, the language and what went wrong inside the app. It never holds names, contacts, location, photos or anything typed. Reports are deleted after 30 days. While the internet is off, a report waits on the device and is deleted after 7 days if it can't be sent.

The full details are in our privacy policy.

Playing on two devices

Two children can play each other on two devices in the same room, over the same home Wi-Fi.

  • Pairing needs a grown-up. One device shows a code; a grown-up scans it with the other device's camera, after the grown-up check.
  • The camera only reads that code. No picture is taken, kept or sent.
  • The pairing code is never sent. Every move, and the few messages that keep the game in step, travel encrypted straight between the two devices. There is no chat and nobody can type a message to the other player.
  • If a friend leaves, the other child sees a kind dinosaur message with buttons to save the game or start a new one.
  • You can switch it off. In For grown-ups, turn off Play on two devices. The option is then greyed out with a Wi-Fi-off sign. Starting a game on two devices always asks a grown-up.

Players: no names needed

Each child can have their own player, and a player is their favourite dinosaur. Nobody types a name, so the app never holds one. If two children pick the same dinosaur, the second gets a number, for example “Queenie 2”.

Progress, stars, stickers and settings stay on the device. They are not backed up and do not move to a new device. Saved games and stories are kept in the device’s Documents folder (Android 10 and later), where you can see them in the Files app; they stay there if the app is removed, until you delete them.

Calm by design

  • No adverts and no accounts. The app never asks for an email address.
  • No timers, no streaks to lose, and no reminders pulling children back.
  • Gentle captures by default: pieces are captured without biting or charging.
  • The 👋 Bye! button ends a session kindly, and the app goes quiet when the screen turns off.
  • Lessons and quiz questions can be read aloud, and an easy-to-read font is one tap away (Aa).
  • The learning quiz between games can be switched off, and is never shown between two-player games.
